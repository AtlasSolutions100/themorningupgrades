L-theanine coffee: what caffeine + L-theanine is, and what the label means

By Koby Stull · Updated October 31, 2026

L-theanine is an amino acid found naturally in tea leaves, and it's often added alongside caffeine because many people describe the combination as smoother than caffeine alone. On a label, “caffeine + L-theanine” means each serving contains both; the Supplement Facts panel says how much. The research is real but small: something to try, not a promise.

What L-theanine is

L-theanine is an amino acid that occurs naturally in tea leaves. It's one reason a cup of tea can feel different from a cup of coffee with similar caffeine. A 2022 review in Frontiers in Nutrition reported average contents of about 6.6 mg per gram in green tea, 6.3 in white, 6.1 in oolong and 5.1 in black tea across 37 varieties. On its own it's sold as a supplement; more often you'll see it next to caffeine.

What “L-theanine coffee” means

Coffee doesn't contain L-theanine on its own; tea does. An L-theanine coffee is coffee with L-theanine added, so each cup has both. You'll find it three ways:

Why it's paired with caffeine

People who use the combination often describe it as smoother than caffeine alone. Researchers have looked at the pair mostly through short lab tests: reaction time, attention tasks, and how alert people say they feel.

What the studies actually say

A 2021 systematic review in Cureus (Sohail et al.) looked at five studies of caffeine and L-theanine together. Several reported faster reaction times or better scores on attention tasks with the combination. The review is also clear about the limits: the studies were very small (as few as five to twenty people), some included only men, and the authors called for more research.

So the honest summary: the pairing is popular and has some encouraging small studies behind it. It is not a guarantee of how you'll feel, and it isn't a substitute for sleep.

Reading a “caffeine + L-theanine” label

  • “Natural caffeine” means caffeine from a plant source, such as coffee beans, rather than made in a lab. It's the same molecule either way; the word tells you where it came from.
  • Amounts are per serving. The Supplement Facts panel lists them for one serving (on ours, one level scoop). If ingredients are grouped in a blend, you may see only the blend's total amount.
  • The FDA line. “These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration” appears on dietary supplements whose labels describe what the product supports. It means what it says: the FDA hasn't reviewed that wording.
  • “Contains caffeine.” L-theanine doesn't reduce the caffeine in a serving, so count it like any coffee.

Counting your caffeine

For most healthy adults, the FDA cites 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects, and lists a 12 oz cup of brewed coffee at roughly 113–247 mg. Sensitivity varies from person to person.

How to try it sensibly

  • Swap, don't stack. Replace a cup of coffee rather than adding caffeine on top.
  • Have it in the morning. It still contains caffeine.
  • Judge it over a week, not a single cup: the taste, the routine, and whether you still reach for a second cup.

Questions

Does L-theanine cancel out caffeine?

No. Adding L-theanine doesn't lower the amount of caffeine in a serving. Count it the same as any other coffee.

Where does L-theanine come from?

It occurs naturally in tea leaves. When it's listed as an ingredient, the product contains it as an added ingredient; the label shows how much per serving.

How much L-theanine is in a cup of tea?

It varies with the tea, how much leaf you use and how long it steeps. A 2022 review averaged roughly 5 to 7 mg per gram of dry tea across 37 varieties.

Can I drink a caffeine + L-theanine coffee in the afternoon?

It still contains caffeine. If caffeine late in the day affects your sleep, keep it to the morning. More on timing an afternoon cup.

Does coffee naturally contain L-theanine?

No. L-theanine occurs naturally in tea leaves. Coffee has it only when it's added, and then the label lists it.

What does “natural caffeine” mean on a label?

Caffeine that comes from a plant, such as coffee beans, rather than being made synthetically. The molecule is the same; the term describes the source.
