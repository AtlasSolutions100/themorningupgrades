What is functional coffee?

By Koby Stull · Updated November 3, 2026

Functional coffee is coffee with something added beyond the coffee itself. MCT powder, L-theanine, extra caffeine, mushroom extracts, protein and collagen are common. There’s no single recipe, so the name tells you little. The ingredient panel tells you more: what each serving holds, and how much.

What gets added

Added ingredientWhat it isWhat to check
MCT oil or powderFats with medium-length chains, usually from coconut or palm kernel oilOil or powder, the carrier in a powder, grams per serving
L-theanineAn amino acid found naturally in tea leavesMilligrams per serving
Added caffeineCaffeine from a plant (“natural”) or made synthetically; the same moleculeThe added milligrams, plus the coffee’s own
Mushroom extractsPowders of lion’s mane, reishi, chaga, cordyceps and othersWhich mushroom, which part, milligrams per serving
Protein or collagenA protein powder mixed into the coffeeGrams of protein and where it comes from
Herbal extractsPlant extracts listed by nameThe plant, the part used, milligrams per serving

Each has its own label to read. For the 2 most common, see what MCT coffee is and L-theanine with caffeine. For mushroom blends, see mushroom coffee vs. MCT coffee.

Food label or supplement label?

Some functional coffees are sold as food and carry a Nutrition Facts panel. Others are sold as dietary supplements and carry a Supplement Facts panel. The FDA does not approve dietary supplements for safety, effectiveness or labeling before they’re sold; it says the companies are responsible for making sure their products are safe and accurately labeled.

That’s why a supplement label that says what a product supports also carries the line “These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration.” It means the FDA hasn’t reviewed that wording.

How to compare 2 functional coffees

  • The amount of each added ingredient per serving. A “proprietary blend” shows only the blend’s total, so you can’t see how much of each is inside.
  • Total caffeine. Added caffeine plus the coffee’s own. How much caffeine instant coffee has.
  • Serving size and servings per bag. Then work out the cost per serving, with shipping. Cost per cup, worked out.
  • How it mixes. Powders stir into hot water; oils need a blender or frother.
  • The “other ingredients” line and the allergen statement. Carriers such as acacia gum or milk protein show up there.

Counting your caffeine

For most healthy adults, the FDA cites 400 mg a day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects. Sensitivity varies. Count every source across the day, functional coffee included.

Questions

Is functional coffee the same as mushroom coffee?

No. Mushroom coffee is 1 kind of functional coffee. Others add MCT, L-theanine, extra caffeine, protein or collagen.

Does functional coffee have caffeine?

Most do, unless it’s made with decaf. Some add caffeine on top of the coffee’s own, so check the panel for the added amount and count both.

Is functional coffee approved by the FDA?

Functional coffees sold as dietary supplements aren’t approved by the FDA before they’re sold. The FDA says supplement companies are responsible for making sure their products are safe and accurately labeled.

What is a proprietary blend?

A group of ingredients listed under 1 name with only the blend’s total amount in milligrams. You can see what’s in it, but not how much of each.
