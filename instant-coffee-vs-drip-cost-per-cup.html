Instant coffee vs. drip coffee: cost per cup

By Koby Stull · Updated October 29, 2026

Cost per cup is the price of the coffee divided by the number of cups it makes. Do that for each option and add the extras you'd actually use: filters, pods, milk, a café detour. Plain instant coffee is usually the cheapest per cup; a café latte the most expensive; drip at home sits in between, depending on the beans.

The formula

Cost per cup = price paid ÷ cups it makes. For instant coffee and pods, the label tells you the servings. For ground coffee, divide the grams in the bag by the grams you use per cup (many people use around 10–15 g for an 8 fl oz cup; weigh yours once to know).

Questions

How do I calculate cost per cup of coffee?

Divide what you paid by the number of cups it makes. For ground coffee, cups = the bag's grams divided by the grams you use per cup.

Is instant coffee cheaper than brewing?

Plain instant coffee usually costs less per cup than ground coffee or pods. Specialty and functional instant coffees can cost more, so run the numbers.

Should I include shipping in cost per cup?

Yes, if you pay it. Add it to the price before dividing.

How many grams of ground coffee per cup?

Many people use around 10 to 15 g for an 8 fl oz cup. Weigh your usual scoop once to get your own number.
