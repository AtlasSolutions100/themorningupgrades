What is MCT coffee?

By Koby Stull · Updated October 30, 2026

MCT coffee is coffee with medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs) added. MCTs are a type of fat, usually made from coconut or palm kernel oil, and they come either as a clear oil you blend into brewed coffee or as a powder you stir into the cup. You notice it as texture: a smoother, creamier body without milk or cream.

What “MCT” means

A triglyceride is three fatty acids attached to a glycerol backbone; it's the basic shape of most fats in food. In a medium-chain triglyceride those fatty acids are a medium length, six to twelve carbon atoms. A 2022 review in the Journal of Food Science and Technology lists them as caproic (C6), caprylic (C8), capric (C10) and lauric (C12) acid. Most of the fat in everyday food has longer chains than that.

Where MCTs come from

Coconut oil and palm kernel oil are the richest natural sources. The same review puts coconut oil at roughly 46–54% lauric acid, 5–10% caprylic acid and 5–8% capric acid; milk fat has small amounts too. Commercial MCT products separate the medium-chain fats out of those oils, so they're more concentrated than the oil they came from. Labels often name the chain lengths a product uses, such as C8 and C10.

MCT oil vs. MCT powder

What it isThe fats as a clear, nearly flavorless liquidMCT oil dried onto a carrier so it disperses in water

Powders differ in what the carrier is and how much MCT a serving holds, so read the label of any powder you compare.

What MCT coffee isn't

It isn't butter coffee, though the two get mixed up: butter coffee is brewed coffee blended with butter or ghee, usually with MCT oil as well (how they compare). And it's still coffee. It has whatever caffeine the coffee has, so count it the same way. MCTs are a fat, so the nutrition or Supplement Facts panel is the place to see what a serving adds.

Questions

Is MCT coffee the same as butter coffee?

No. Butter coffee is brewed coffee blended with butter or ghee and usually MCT oil. MCT coffee has MCT oil or powder added and no butter. Butter coffee needs a blender; MCT powder coffee only needs a spoon.

Is MCT oil the same as coconut oil?

No. MCT oil is made from coconut or palm kernel oil but keeps only the medium-chain fats, so it stays liquid and has almost no taste. Coconut oil is mostly lauric acid, tastes of coconut and turns solid in a cool kitchen.

Do I need a blender to make MCT coffee?

Only with MCT oil, which otherwise floats on top. MCT powder is made to dissolve in hot water, so a spoon is enough.

Does MCT coffee have caffeine?

Yes, unless it's made with decaf. Ours has 100 mg of added natural caffeine per scoop, plus the instant coffee's own; see the Supplement Facts panel.

Can I make MCT coffee iced?

Yes. Dissolve the powder in about 2 oz of hot water first, then pour it over ice and top up with cold water or milk. Cold water alone leaves clumps.
