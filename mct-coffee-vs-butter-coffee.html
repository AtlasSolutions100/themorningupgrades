MCT coffee vs. butter coffee: what's the difference?

By Koby Stull · Updated October 28, 2026

Butter coffee is brewed coffee blended with butter or ghee, usually plus MCT oil; MCT coffee is coffee with MCT oil or MCT powder added and no butter. Butter coffee needs a blender and comes out thick and frothy. MCT powder coffee is stirred with a spoon and comes out smooth and lightly creamy.

What butter coffee is

Butter coffee is brewed coffee blended with unsalted butter or ghee, and usually a spoonful of MCT oil. Blending matters: without it, the fat floats on top in a slick. Done right, you get a frothy, latte-like cup.

What MCTs are

MCT stands for medium-chain triglycerides: fats whose fatty-acid chains are a medium length, roughly 6 to 12 carbons. Commercial MCT oil is usually made from coconut or palm kernel oil. It's clear, nearly flavorless and stays liquid at room temperature. More on what MCT coffee is.

Oil vs. powder

MCT powder is MCT oil that has been dried onto a carrier so it dissolves in water instead of sitting on top of it. That's the whole trick: no blender, no oily film, and it gives the cup a creamier body. Check the label of any powder for the carrier and serving size; they vary.

Which one fits your mornings

If you like a weekend ritual and a rich, almost dessert-like cup, butter coffee is worth the blender. If your mornings are a race, a powder that dissolves in a stir is the one you'll actually keep making. Either way, the fat content is real: read the nutrition panel and decide what fits how you eat.

Questions

Can I use coconut oil instead of MCT oil in butter coffee?

Yes. Coconut oil works but tastes of coconut and turns solid when it cools, so blend it well and drink it warm.

Do I need a blender for MCT powder?

No. MCT powder is made to dissolve in hot water; a spoon is enough.

Which one has more fat?

Usually butter coffee, since it combines butter and oil. Compare the nutrition panels of what you actually use.

Can either one be made iced?

MCT powder can: dissolve it in a little hot water, then add ice. Butter coffee is best hot; the butter firms up in a cold drink.
